You don't "make" your heart beat any more than you "make" the sun shine.

You are not a small, isolated being but a localized expression of the entire cosmos sampling itself subjectively.

By realizing that circulating blood and the shining sun are equally involuntary and spontaneous, you begin to see yourself as a continuation of universal phenomena rather than apart from them. This fosters a sense of profound kinship with all existence. The voluntary and involuntary bleed into one another.

Premise (P1): In most (if not all) conscious experiences, there is a universal sense of “I-ness” (i.e., the “I” that is aware).

Premise (P2): This sense of “I-ness” remains identical in kind regardless of the content of the experience (e.g., happy thoughts, sad thoughts, memories)—thus, it is independent of specific content.

Premise (P3): Any universal feature of consciousness that exists independently of particular contents implies an underlying continuity or unity of that feature.

  • Justification: If it does not change with specific instances, then we have reason to believe it is one “singular” phenomenon expressing itself in multiple contexts (analogous to how space remains one continuous entity despite being “partitioned” by objects).
    Conclusion (C1): Therefore, the recurring sense of “I” across different (and even newly born) conscious beings reflects the continuity of a single underlying consciousness, rather than separate, isolated “I’s.”

  • In other words, the same “I-ness” is manifesting anew, not being created from nothing each time.

  1. A Universal Memory

Discussion

Detailed version:

Premise 1 (Empirical): Consciousness exhibits consistent patterns across all known instances:

  • Neuroscientific Evidence: Neuroscience identifies similar neural correlates of consciousness across species, particularly the default mode network (DMN). When the DMN is active, self-referential thinking and introspection occur; when disrupted, these capacities diminish, demonstrating a causal relationship between neural activity and conscious experience.

  • Structural Features: All conscious experiences share three fundamental characteristics:

    • Unity: Experiences are integrated into a single field of awareness.
    • Temporality: Experiences occur in a perceived sequence.
    • First-person Perspective: Experiences are inherently subjective.
      These features persist across altered states (e.g., meditation, psychedelics), varying levels of consciousness (sleep, anesthesia), and species with similar neural architecture.

Premise 2 (Phenomenological): Pure awareness—the capacity for experience itself—can be distinguished from its contents:

  • Definition: Pure awareness is the capacity to experience, independent of specific mental content (thoughts, sensations, emotions).

  • Evidence:

    • Meditation research documents states of "witness consciousness"—bare awareness without conceptual content.

    • Clinical cases show preserved self-awareness despite profound changes:

      - *Amnesia patients* maintain moment-to-moment awareness despite lost memories.
- *Split-brain patients* retain unified consciousness despite divided cognitive processes.

The stability of this observing awareness across radical changes in mental content demonstrates its independence from particular contents.

Premise 3 (Logical): A phenomenon is fundamental when it is:

  1. Universal across all instances,
  2. Irreducible—attempting to analyze it into parts destroys its nature (e.g., awareness cannot be divided), and
  3. Identity-Preserving despite changing contents.
    Fundamentality implies unity because:

  • Multiple instances would require differentiating features.

  • Differentiating features imply complexity, which contradicts fundamentality.
    Bridge Premise: Fundamental phenomena in nature exhibit unity:

  • Space-time appears divided but remains a mathematically unified field.

  • Quantum fields underlie seemingly distinct particles.

  • Fundamental forces (e.g., gravity, electromagnetism) manifest in varied ways but stem from unified origins.
    Applicability to Consciousness: The principles of fundamentality and unity are metaphysical, not strictly physical, and therefore apply logically to consciousness.

Supporting Evidence:

  1. Biological Continuity: Life arises as a continuation of a prior life. Consciousness, being tied to biological processes, likely follows similar patterns of continuity.
  2. Theoretical Support: Integrated Information Theory (IIT) mathematically demonstrates how consciousness could be unified yet locally manifesting, mirroring fundamental physical fields.
  3. Phenomenological Consistency: Mystical experiences, deep meditation, and near-death reports across cultures consistently point to an underlying unity of awareness.
    Analogy: Just as space appears fragmented by objects but remains singular and continuous, consciousness may appear divided by individual experiences while being fundamentally unified.

Conclusion: Consciousness is more likely a unified field that manifests locally because:

  • It exhibits features of fundamental phenomena (universality, irreducibility, identity-preservation).
  • Fundamental phenomena are necessarily unified.
  • Multiple lines of empirical, logical, and phenomenological evidence support this unity.
    While probabilistic rather than deductive, this conclusion offers a rational basis for accepting consciousness's underlying unity.

I say this while acknowledging the limits of current understanding.
