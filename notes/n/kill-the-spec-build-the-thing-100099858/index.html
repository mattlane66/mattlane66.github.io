Kill the Spec, Build the Thing

Functional specs are fantasies. Reality is built, not imagined.

Words on paper won’t code themselves into existence.

Specs appease egos more than solve problems.

Tough decisions don’t fit neatly onto pages. True costs remain hidden.

Agreement in text is illusion.

Everyone nods, but nobody sees the same vision. Reality clarifies differences.

Specs force critical choices when information is scarce.

Real knowledge comes later, in building, testing, using.

Dead documents fill bins, not browsers.

If paperwork doesn’t transform into a product, don’t produce it.

Unread specs serve no one.

Teams thrive through conversation and prototypes, not paragraphs.

Feature lists breed clutter, not clarity.

Bullets are cheap; consequences aren’t.

Locked-in specs kill adaptability.

Products evolve when teams embrace change, not resist it.

Tell stories, quick and vivid.

Short narratives launch action. Long specs stall progress.

Prototypes over documents.

Sketches and screens create clarity. Words alone breed confusion.

Write less. Build more.

Great products aren’t written—they’re grown.
