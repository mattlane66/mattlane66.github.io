Coordination is a problem of fit

The more independently people work, the harder it becomes to ensure their contributions fit together.

We tend to solve this with more communication: meetings, updates, documentation, and approvals. But knowing what others are doing isn't the same as making the work coherent.

A better approach starts with a shared understanding of what we're trying to achieve, what already exists, and what an acceptable solution must satisfy.

From there:

  • Coordinate intentions, not tasks. Understand what people are trying to achieve and why.
  • Check fit in both directions. Does the proposed solution satisfy the requirements? Does every part of the solution serve a requirement?
  • Find relationships between efforts. Look for duplication, dependencies, contradictions, and opportunities to combine work.
  • Reconcile early. Resolve incompatibilities while work is taking shape, not after it's finished.
  • Involve people selectively. Bring others in when their judgment, authority, or collaboration is needed.
  • Verify the whole. Individually correct contributions can still produce a result that doesn't work together.

The goal isn't to make everyone coordinate more.

It's to let people work independently without losing coherence.

Fit isn't established once. It has to be maintained as things change.
