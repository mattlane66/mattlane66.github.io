Rational Agency Science

The study of justified action under uncertainty

I suspect one of the most important fields of the next twenty years doesn't yet exist as a unified discipline. I'd call it Rational Agency Science.

In a world of superintelligence and autonomous machines, the challenge may shift from what intelligence can accomplish to what it should pursue, why, and how we know.

The chain

Intelligent action can be examined as a chain:

Objective → Belief → Decision → Action → Outcome → Attribution → Revision

Each link can fail in a different way:

  • A system can reason correctly from false assumptions.
  • It can optimize an unjustified objective.
  • It can execute a sound decision poorly.
  • It can attribute failure to the wrong cause.
  • It can achieve its goal while producing unacceptable consequences.

Greater intelligence alone doesn't resolve these problems: even a precisely specified objective cannot establish that the objective is justified, the observations trustworthy, or the right causes identified.

AI alignment, cybernetics, and decision science already address parts of this problem. Rational Agency Science would study the integrity of the whole chain, including how failures propagate between its parts.

Four jobs

The field would integrate existing disciplines around four connected functions:

  • Justify: Philosophy examines what is worth pursuing, what we can know, and what justifies our choices. Institutional governance establishes authority and accountability.
  • Choose and coordinate: Decision theory evaluates actions under uncertainty and constraints. Mechanism design addresses cooperation and competing incentives.
  • Act and verify: Control theory translates decisions into action and corrects deviations. Verification tests whether requirements are met.
  • Explore and learn: Creative reasoning expands what we consider possible. Practical experimentation tests our understanding against reality. Causal inference and credit assignment help explain outcomes and determine what should change.

These aren't sequential activities. Each can reveal errors in the others.

A grid under stress

Imagine an electrical grid facing a predicted 100-megawatt shortage during a heatwave.

An intelligent system decides to supply 40 megawatts from batteries and reduce industrial demand by 60 megawatts, protecting essential services.

But industrial customers deliver only 40 megawatts of reduction. The system detects the reported shortfall and increases battery output by 20 megawatts.

Feedback control responds to the immediate problem. The harder question is what the system should learn.

Did a customer fail to respond? Were instructions never received? Was the delivered reduction measured incorrectly? Were the incentives inadequate?

The reported shortfall alone doesn't establish its cause.

Three distinct questions emerge:

  • Causal: What contributed to the outcome?
  • Epistemic: What evidence justifies that attribution?
  • Normative: Who or what should be held responsible, and what should change?

These questions are related but not interchangeable. Something can contribute causally to a failure without anyone being morally responsible for it.

Each explanation requires different evidence and potentially different action. An unreliable customer response may call for revised resource commitments. A lost instruction calls for better communications and acknowledgments. A measurement error calls for better instrumentation and validation; it may even mean the extra battery output wasn't needed. Inadequate incentives call for redesigned participation rules.

Creative reasoning might reveal an entirely different way to manage peak demand. Experimentation could establish whether it works.

The system must be capable not only of correcting its actions, but also of questioning the assumptions, objectives, and arrangements that produced them.

The ambition

Intelligence expands what is possible. Judgment determines what is worth pursuing. Agency turns that judgment into action and remains open to correction.

We need justified objectives, creative possibilities, sound decisions, effective action, accurate attribution, and the ability to revise each of them.

That is the ambition of Rational Agency Science.
