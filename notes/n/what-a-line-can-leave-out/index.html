What a Line Can Leave Out

Frédéric Forest can suggest a whole person with the bend of a wrist, the slope of a shoulder, and almost nothing else.

His drawings are sparse, but they don't feel unfinished. A contour gives us weight. A gesture suggests what happened just before, or what might happen next. The missing details are supplied by the person looking.

The space around the line is doing part of the drawing. A face can remain blank without losing its presence. Two hands can establish a relationship without the people attached to them.

Forest describes drawing as trying to find the perfect line. He makes repeated attempts rather than correcting them with an eraser. The hand must be guided, but also allowed to move. He calls it a balance between control and instinct.

That is what makes the work interesting to me. Using fewer marks is only subtraction. Knowing which marks can carry the image is judgment. The line needs to be precise enough to suggest something, and open enough to let us see more than it says.

The point is not to draw less.
It is to leave nothing essential out.
