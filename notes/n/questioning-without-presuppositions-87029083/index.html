Questioning Without Presuppositions

- **Core Idea**: Don’t embed unverified assumptions directly in your main question.
- **Practical Tip**: Turn any unverified assumption into (1) a separate question to establish the fact, or (2) a conditional (“If P then Q?”).
- **Result**: You maintain logical rigor (no hidden premises) yet still can explore scenarios where a presupposition might *or* might not hold.
This is *QUESTION SCIENCE*.

**Treat questions with unestablished presuppositions as conditional or hypothetical** rather than outright *un-askable.* In other words:

  1. **Explicitly Acknowledge the Presupposition**
  • Instead of “Do we punish Bob for not returning the library book?” you might say:

“*If* Bob indeed checked out a book and has not returned it, *then* do we punish Bob?”
- This way, you **surface** the presupposition (that Bob checked out a book) instead of **embedding** it in the question.
2. **Use Conditional or Hypothetical Questions**

- Formally, pose questions of the form “If P(x, then Q(x)?” rather than presupposing P(x) is true.
- This approach is recognized in logic and philosophy as *conditional reasoning* or *hypothetical reasoning.*
- When the premise P(x) is verified, you can continue with Q(x). Otherwise, you discard that line of questioning or reframe it.
3. **Meta-Questions**

- If you need to explore whether P(x) is true, ask a *meta-question* first: “Is P(x) true?” This clarifies the basis for subsequent lines of questioning.
- Only if the meta-question verifies P(x), you then proceed to “Given P(x), Q(x)?”
4. **Interrogative Logic**

- In *interrogative logic* (developed by Jaakko Hintikka and others), you systematically separate *search questions* (to verify presuppositions) from *logical inference steps.*
- Example:

- (1) **Search question**: “Is there a King of France?”
- (2) **If answer is yes**: “Then what is his name?”
- (3) **If answer is no**: “Then does the phrase ‘King of France’ refer to a historical or imaginary role?”
By making each presupposition an explicit *if/then* (conditional) or raising it as a separate question, you combine **logical rigor** with **practical flexibility**. You avoid burying unverified assumptions inside your main question while allowing your inquiry to explore uncertain territory—rather than stopping dead whenever a presupposition is not yet established.
