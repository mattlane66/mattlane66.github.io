Practical Math

I think we should be teaching optimization word problems in math class to kids sooner than we think (closer to 10…not starting at 16 years old…Perhaps even sooner).

Optimization word problems incorporate proportionality, probability, and feedback loops into a framework that is both practical and metaphorically powerful. In other words: useful. Its practicality ranges from personal health and finance to time management/resource allocation and scaling a business.

Optimization Problem Example:

Problem: You are planning a road trip with friends and want to minimize total cost (including fuel and snacks) while balancing time constraints and uncertainty around fuel efficiency due to variable road conditions.

The constraints are:

  1. The trip distance is 500 miles.
  2. Your car averages 25 miles per gallon (mpg), but this may vary between 20-30 mpg due to weather and traffic.
  3. Gas costs $4 per gallon, and you expect to spend $2 per hour on snacks during driving and rest stops.
  4. You must rest for 30 minutes after every 3 hours of driving to avoid fatigue.
  5. Your average driving speed is 60 miles per hour (mph).
  6. You have a maximum of 10 hours for the trip, including rest stops.
    How can you minimize the total cost of the trip, including fuel and snacks, while adhering to the time constraint of 10 hours and accounting for uncertainty in fuel efficiency due to variable road conditions?
