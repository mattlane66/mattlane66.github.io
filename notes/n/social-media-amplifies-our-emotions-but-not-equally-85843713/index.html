Social media amplifies our emotions, but not equally.

Research shows that while most posts express joy or contentment, anger travels faster and further. Why? While joyful content tends to stay within close social circles, outrage bridges communities, spreading rapidly across weak social ties between networks (http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.01924).

The solution isn't just better moderation - it's perhaps smarter amplification. Using AI to increase the visibility of thoughtful, constructive voices while educating users about participatory moderation principles, platforms, and their users, can help shape stimulating and healthy online spaces.

The Playbook for Better Digital Discourse

Our goal is simple but vital: to learn from each other, gather new perspectives, and find joy in the process. Here's how:

Lead with kindness. When disagreeing—and disagreement is essential—craft your words to be heard, not just spoken. This isn't about diluting your message but ensuring it reaches its audience.

Reserve outrage for what truly matters. When you encounter genuine bigotry or hatred, address it directly. But first, seek to understand others as they wish to be understood, not as you assume them to be.

Ask more than you declare. Questions open minds that statements leave closed. Share your unique perspective, but pair it with genuine curiosity. What might others see that you've missed? What assumptions hide in your certainties?

Be an active participant in truth-seeking. Build on others' ideas, connect disparate threads, and draw out quieter voices. The quality of our discourse depends on everyone's participation.

Stay light while tackling heavy topics. Choose authenticity over solemnity, and let wit serve wisdom.

Because we have multiple businesses in a pluralistic society, each should have the autonomy to set a “tasteful” standard that aligns with a widely shared moral framework—opposing oppression, supporting dignity, and fostering stimulating dialogue.

A carefully calibrated approach to amplification—where robust debate is allowed yet hateful or malicious content is addressed and named—protects free expression while cultivating a respectful, vibrant, and meaningful community.

Sticking to the baseline is for governmental systems. Overreaching is for the misguided.

Beyond the Baseline: Detailed AI and Community Moderation Blueprint with Scientific Foundation

These guidelines draw on established research to craft online spaces that are safer, fairer, and grounded in proven principles—beyond minimal legal requirements.

1. Hate Speech & Dehumanization Train AI classifiers on recognized slurs, pejorative metaphors, and subtle coded language. Provide explicit, user-facing definitions and enforce graduated penalties (warnings, then suspensions).

  • Scientific Basis: Dehumanization correlates with aggression and reduced empathy (Haslam, 2006). Social Identity Theory shows how in-group/out-group divides encourage hostility (Tajfel, 1979).
    2. Misinformation Pin real-time AI “fact snapshots” to viral posts, supplemented by volunteer “fact patrols” who swiftly flag high-risk claims. Provide tutorials on verifying sources and identifying logical fallacies.

  • Scientific Basis: False news spreads faster than accurate information, partly due to emotional appeal (Vosoughi et al., 2018). Confirmation bias and motivated reasoning increase susceptibility (Nickerson, 1998).
    3. Harassment & Bullying Require identity verification for severe infractions. Use conversational analytics to detect repeated insults or dogpiling, triggering cooling-off periods and mental health support hotlinks.

  • Scientific Basis: Cyberbullying correlates with anxiety, depression, and suicidal ideation (Kowalski et al., 2014). Hostile behavior escalates in unchecked digital environments (Cheng et al., 2017).
    4. Polarizing or Divisive Content Downrank posts algorithmically designed to spark sensational outrage. Encourage structured debate with dedicated community moderators, rewarding calm, reasoned discourse.

  • Scientific Basis: Emotional contagion amplifies negativity and deepens divides (Kramer et al., 2014). Polarizing content erodes trust in institutions and fosters radicalization (various political science studies).
    5. Excessively Graphic or Violent Content Automatically blur disturbing imagery and provide contextual advisories. Community moderators review borderline cases; and offer user controls to skip or limit repeated exposure.

  • Scientific Basis: Violent imagery can induce trauma-like symptoms (Weathers et al., 1993). Prolonged exposure leads to desensitization and aggression (Anderson et al., 2003).
    6. Content Exploiting Vulnerable Groups Use predictive AI to detect manipulative wording or suspicious fundraising. Implement specialized reporting flows for scams, including child exploitation. Collaborate with advocacy groups for immediate escalation.

  • Scientific Basis: Certain populations (e.g., the elderly) are more susceptible to manipulation (Schaepkens et al., 2021). Fraud often results in significant financial harm (various NGO/government reports).
    7. Toxic Extremism Flag repeated extremist rhetoric; employ multi-step verification for high-risk accounts. Document removal rationales publicly. Invest in counter-narratives, including testimonies from reformed extremists.

  • Scientific Basis: Repeated exposure to extremist content accelerates radicalization (Borum, 2011). Echo chambers reinforce extreme viewpoints (Sunstein, 2001).
    8. Algorithmic Bias & Content Amplification Regularly publish ranking metrics and empower users with customizable feed preferences (e.g., “fact-first” or “balanced news”). Conduct external audits to expose hidden biases in training data.

  • Scientific Basis: Algorithmic feedback loops create filter bubbles (Pariser, 2011). Biased machine learning perpetuates societal inequalities (Noble, 2018).
    By combining robust AI detection, transparent policies, and user empowerment—anchored in peer-reviewed research—platforms can cultivate healthier, more trustworthy digital ecosystems.

Core Principles for Going Beyond the Baseline

  • Prosocial Behavior: Encourage cooperation, empathy, and constructive dialogue.
  • Harm Reduction: Mitigate indirect or cumulative harms (e.g., misinformation’s impact on systems health).
  • Global Standards: Reflect universal moral principles while accounting for cultural variability.
  • Transparency: Communicate community and AI moderation standards to align user expectations with platform goals.
