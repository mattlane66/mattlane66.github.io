2026-10-01 · Judgment & knowledge

One way to understand taste is to notice its absence.

You lack taste when you can tell something is off but cannot say precisely what, why it matters, or what you would change. You reach for another perspective because you do not yet trust your own judgment.

Taste closes that gap.

It is the ability to perceive meaningful differences, understand the choices producing them, know the standards and context those choices operate within, and judge which tradeoffs best serve the thing being made.

Good taste is not certainty or preference. It is disciplined discrimination: knowing what matters, what does not, what belongs, what gets in the way, and why.

And it remains accountable to reality. You make the choice deliberately, compare it against alternatives, observe what it actually does, and revise your judgment when the effect is not the one you intended.

Taste is preference that has learned to explain itself, reproduce itself, and correct itself.
