The Compounding Value Model

People don't always know what they want. Sometimes they recognize a problem. Sometimes a new possibility changes what they want.

A product can answer existing demand or reveal demand that wasn't apparent before.

1. How value compounds

1. Find or create an opportunity.

Understand what people struggle to do, want to experience, or have yet to imagine.

2. Make something worth choosing.

Deliver an outcome or experience people value more than the available alternatives, including doing nothing.

3. Look for evidence of demand.

  • They return.
  • They'd miss it.
  • They tell others.
  • They pay.

What people do tells us more than what they say. No single signal proves demand.

4. Make the economics work.

Create more value than you charge for. Capture enough to cover the costs of acquiring, serving, and retaining customers.

5. Build advantages that grow with use.

Each cycle can leave something valuable behind: deeper integration, a stronger network, better knowledge, or lower unit costs.

An advantage becomes a moat only when competitors struggle to reproduce or overcome it.

6. Turn those advantages into more value.

Use what accumulates to improve the product or its economics. Better value can attract more demand. More demand can strengthen the advantage.

The loop compounds when each turn makes the next one stronger.

2. How to cause what matters

What matters What to do
They return. Deliver value reliably whenever the occasion arises.
They'd miss it. Become meaningfully better than what they could use instead.
They tell others. Create an experience or result worth sharing, and make sharing easy.
They pay. Make the value worth its price. Test that belief through actual purchases.
Switching costs. Build useful integrations, context, and capabilities that become costly to replace.
Network effects. Make each additional participant increase the value others receive.
Learning effects. Turn feedback from real outcomes into improvements competitors cannot easily replicate.
Economies of scale. Build operations that lower unit costs as volume grows.

The principle

Make value compound.

Create something people choose, return to, recommend, and willingly pay for.

Make the economics sustainable.

Make success strengthen the product and the business.

Make that advantage difficult to copy.

Growth makes a business bigger. Compounding makes it stronger as it grows.
