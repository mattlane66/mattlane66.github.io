Judgment skills are central to critical thinking: deciding what’s true, what matters, and what should be done. These forms of metacognitive knowledge should be taught to every kid. These are high-stakes agency skills that can atrophy if delegated.

A citizen who can’t form an independent perspective is easy to steer—by ideology, by media, or by AI.

Short list:

Reading & interpretation

Extract claims, infer meaning, evaluate framing, detect rhetoric, notice omissions and ambiguity.

Argumentation

Claim → reasons → assumptions → evidence → best objections → revised view.

Epistemic judgment

Calibration, source evaluation, bias/motivated reasoning detection, standards of proof (including correlation vs causation).

Uncertainty & quantitative literacy

Base rates, expected impact, orders of magnitude, basic statistical literacy, feedback loops and unintended consequences.

Practical wisdom

When rules apply, context sensitivity, second-order effects, tradeoffs under constraints, situational judgment.

Moral & political reasoning

Legitimacy, rights, justice, welfare, liberty/equality tensions, how to justify collective power and policy.

Self-governance

Embodied awareness (sleep, stress, hunger, energy), sustainable habits, distress tolerance, impulse control, self-knowledge that supports clear thinking.

Social fluency & care

Empathy and perspective-taking, conversational norms (listening/turn-taking), respect signals, gratitude, disagreement without contempt, apology and repair, everyday etiquette as coordination.

Aesthetic judgment

Taste and quality discernment: noticing beauty, understanding why something works, recognizing craftsmanship, standards beyond trend-following.

———

Critical thinking isn’t only critiquing ideas; it’s also generating better ones.

Imagination is how you produce alternatives, counterfactuals, and “other ways x could work,” which is essential for problem-solving and creation.
