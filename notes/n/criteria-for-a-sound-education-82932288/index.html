Criteria for a sound education

- **Independent Inquiry and Creation:** A good education fosters the ability to ask questions, think critically, and create new ideas without relying on external guidance.
- **Resourcefulness:** It equips individuals with the knowledge and skills to find information, formulate questions, and pursue their learning paths.
- **Critical Thinking:** A good education encourages questioning of established ideas and doctrines, promoting independent thought and the development of original perspectives.
- **Lifelong Learning:** It cultivates a love of learning and a desire to continuously expand one's knowledge and understanding throughout life.
- **Collaboration and Solidarity:** A good education emphasizes the importance of working with others, sharing knowledge, and building a community of learners.
And this excellence reveals itself in a **clear intellect** and a **noble character**.

Trade school/training programs can certainly benefit from those attributes while developing a specific durable skill remains the top priority.

**Tweaks to the public curriculum could be more personalized and engaging:**

1. **Mathematics**: Math classes should prioritize practical applications, ensuring students see how mathematical concepts connect to real-world problems and everyday life. By linking abstract theories to tangible outcomes, students develop both understanding and utility.
2. **Writing**: Writing assignments should balance personal expression with technical skill-building. By crafting assignments that are relevant to students' lives and interests, educators can foster articulate, confident communicators who are adept at both creative and technical writing.
3. **History**: History education should focus on teaching students how to research, analyze, and verify facts, rather than merely memorizing them. This emphasis on critical thinking and information literacy equips students to navigate and interpret the complexities of the modern world.
4. **Science**: Science classes should nurture curiosity about the natural world while teaching the scientific method—how to test hypotheses and evaluate results. Personalized approaches can deepen engagement by connecting lessons to students’ interests and experiences.
5. **Arts**: Every student should engage in an art form of their choosing—be it drawing, music, poetry, or literature. This practice develops creative skills, fosters self-expression, and cultivates discipline in the pursuit of taste-building.
6. **Physical Education**: Beyond physical activity, students should learn about personal health, including preventive measures, longevity strategies, and safe practices to maintain and improve their well-being. This focus equips students with the knowledge to make informed choices about their health.
7. **Collaboration Across Disciplines**: Every class should incorporate collaborative elements, teaching students how to interact constructively, build relationships, work in teams, and resolve conflicts. These skills are essential for developing emotional intelligence and preparing for personal and professional life.
It’s not just about “learning about” but about “becoming”/“thinking like”.

We don’t just learn about history but become historians. So on and so forth.
