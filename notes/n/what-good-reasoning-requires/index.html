What Good Reasoning Requires

Good reasoning is not simply about having enough evidence. It's about ensuring that our conclusions are justified by our evidence, that our assumptions are examined, and that we're willing to revise our beliefs when better evidence emerges.

Five questions help us do that:

  1. What is the problem, and what do we actually know?
  2. What might explain it, and what else could?
  3. What are we assuming, and does our reasoning hold?
  4. What would show us we're wrong?
  5. What should we now believe, revise, or investigate?

These questions don't just help us find answers. They help us identify and describe problems more accurately. When a problem seems insoluble, we should examine the question itself. Have we framed it correctly? What does the framing assume or exclude? Sometimes the way forward isn't a better answer, but a better question.
