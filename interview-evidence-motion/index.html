Four questions / four methods

the question needs.

The Scaled Interview Data Assistant is an inspectable method, agent instructions, templates, and deterministic checks—not an autonomous researcher. Its router recommends the smallest eligible method, and human review gates control whether interpretations can be treated as reviewed.

01 / CORE A→B

What happened in a real decision?

Reconstruct a bounded struggle, previous workaround, tipping context, action, desired progress and observed result. A missing outcome stays unknown.

02 / MECHANISM MAP

Why might decisions differ?

Compare real contrasting cases against rival mechanisms. Seek counterexamples; do not mistake case counts for population estimates or causation.

03 / SIMULATION + SSR

What alternative is worth testing?

Use explicitly synthetic responses to stress-test counterfactuals. Semantic similarity ratings describe model text, not actual buying probabilities.

04 / LEAD USER

What need might emerge next?

Investigate an important trend, then test whether advanced users are ahead of it and expect unusually high benefit. A candidate is not a qualified Lead User.

The research-to-design boundary. The workflow can propose source-grounded current conditions (x), participant intent, conditional mechanisms (M) and separate emerging questions. It cannot accept a product team's objective, declare requirements or select a solution. Those are downstream design judgments.
About this example.

The animated account uses the repository's published mattress casebook (reviewed November 22, 2026): a licensed workshop interview plus distinct public first-person accounts. Original source: 279 recorded speaker turns, 28 selected excerpts, one historic buyer. Cross-case comparison: eight source-account histories. Its simulation has no matching independent human holdout and its Lead User desk SCOUT qualified no Lead Users. The analysis is source-grounded but has not received independent human research approval. This animation paraphrases the reports rather than presenting invented interview quotations.
