First, change the question.

The interesting move was not simply choosing Stacks. It was changing what counted as value: from finding a sound to finding a musical idea worth developing.

THE STARTING QUESTION

How do we help creators find better sounds, faster?

Search and browsing treat the individual sound as the unit of value. That framing makes improving discovery the obvious answer.

About the model.

This is a retrospective reconstruction of a product decision, not a transcription of the original discovery process. The green nodes are deliberately written as customer needs; where interviews are not available, they are interpretations and hypotheses—not attributed customer quotes.

What the historical record establishes

Splice explored different boundaries for creation: improving discovery, curated starting material, a guided creator, or a fuller composing environment. The product case documents the choice and the later importance of making results steerable.

The timeline matters: CoSo launched first, with Create and subsequent capabilities evolving later. A later feature does not prove the original need was discovered in the same way.

Methods behind the model

Opportunity underwriting framed the economic questions; lead-user research informed how to search for advanced needs; decision-episode analysis informed need language; and behavioral simulation supplied guardrails on counterfactual claims. Missing studies are not shown as completed findings.
