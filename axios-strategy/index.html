Test whether customers will pay Axios to keep research up to date.

PROPOSAL · NOT AN AXIOS INITIATIVE
TODAY

Axios Pro Deals already provides reporting, an AI research assistant, and a dataset of more than 60,000 transactions. Customers still direct individual research sessions.

PROPOSED

Research that keeps running

A customer defines a recurring task once. The service watches specified subjects, updates a sourced report, preserves earlier findings, and sends only the changes.

WHO MIGHT BUY

Pro Deals teams with repeated deal, competitor, or market-monitoring work; then smaller teams that cannot justify a dedicated analyst.

WHAT TO RUN

Enroll 5–10 customers in a proposed six-week paid pilot. Compare delegated recurring jobs with their existing Pro and manual workflow.

WHAT MUST MOVE

Incremental paid renewals and contribution per completed job, supported by evidence of weekly use and less manual work.

WHEN TO STOP

Do not scale if customers won't pay more, material developments are missed, sources cannot be verified, or service costs exceed the value delivered.

Why it may open new demand: people who cannot afford a research function may pay for a bounded, recurring job—rather than another article, search session, or dashboard. This is a hypothesis to test against AI research and monitoring substitutes, not a proven Blue Ocean market.
THE STRATEGIC MOVE

Don't launch eight unrelated products.

Axios’s announced 2028 plan already has a foundation: human-vetted journalism, a planned Local Super System, role-specific products, Axios Direct, and more in-person gatherings. The recommendation above tests a single new paid job: maintaining research over time. The other bets are enabling capabilities or adjacent experiments, not eight independent product launches.

BLUE OCEAN HYPOTHESIS

Sell completed information work, not more content.

Start with small professional teams that need recurring research but cannot staff a monitoring function. Test whether Axios can do that work more accurately and at lower total cost than their current collection of subscriptions, alerts, and manual reporting.

Not a proven blue ocean: substitutes already include AI search, competitive-intelligence platforms, and Reuters/LSEG feeds.
