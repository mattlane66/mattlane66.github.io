Map the argument

Observed: In the same 45-minute interval, 30 entrances occurred: 24 through the front, six through the side. The counter reading rose by 24. A repeat entrance appears in the observer's record.

Observed / reported: One visitor says she would wait; her bus schedule gives context. This is an individual account, not an estimate of population effects.

Inferred: The discrepancy warrants investigating instrument behavior; even a complete entrance count is not a distinct-person count. These are separate measurement limitations.

Explicit proposed decision criterion: The narrator proposes weighing effects on visitors against the cost of opening at nine. This is a proposed value premise; the council’s actual rule and the relevant costs and effects are unknown.

Conclusion: The existing counter alone is insufficient. Gather more appropriate evidence. No final opening-hours recommendation follows from the observed interval alone.

Strengthened policy, separately labeled: A precautionary rule could justify retaining current hours during study, provided the cost and feasibility of delay are assessed. That premise and policy are not part of the narrator's stated recommendation.

When good advice conflicts

Check the facts of the story

Fictional setting, one library, one staff member, one observer, one visitor who uses a wheelchair, another who re-enters. Two entrances; the counter’s detection coverage is unverified. The two doors open into the reading room at opposite ends of the lending desk. The narrator notes an earlier reading, then observes from a seat opposite the desk. A sheet headed Visitors is later relabeled Entrances. The observer sees an anomaly at 9:10 and establishes a matched observation period at 9:15–10:00. A unique-person total is not established. The ending recalls a scene that took place before the narrator wrote that afternoon.

The narrative is fictional. All particulars and quotations are invented as part of that authorized fictional world, not presented as evidence about a real library. Subsequent comparisons must preserve that world unless a change is expressly identified.

Audit limitations

All 18 primitives, 40 concepts and 20 named patterns retain their teaching routes. All 203 source cards now have individual authored checks with a restraint case and source locator. The concept ledger distinguishes direct demonstrations, comparisons and separate exercises. The individual checks were written from the cards’ mechanisms, exceptions and example rationales; they are not a certification that every original example, counterexample and exception has been reproduced or exhaustively validated. Human learning remains untested.
